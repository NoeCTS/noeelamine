An archive, not a CV. Black ground, Klein blue, and photographs left in their own colour. The texture is film grain, and a dot screen that resolves into your own drawing rather than repeating. This sheet is built in the language it proposes.


  
  

    

      01
      
The opening

      Scroll to develop
    

    
You liked the flower opening on Pangaia, so this is the equivalent built from your own material. The site opens on a photograph that does not exist yet: it is a field of characters. As you scroll, the character grid gets finer until the image resolves into itself. Scrolling is developing.

    
It runs in three stages, because characters are monochrome and dropping straight into a hard yellow photograph was a jolt. Characters, then black and white, then colour arrives last. That way colour is the thing the opening earns rather than something it starts with, and it sets up the rule that the interface stays monochrome while the photographs carry the colour.

      02
      
Palette

      Black, white, grey, Klein
    

    
Klein blue is not used as a small accent. That is what everyone does with it and it wastes the colour. It is used the way Klein used it: as a field. Whole blocks of it, with white type on top, against black and grey. Everything else is monochrome, so the blue is the only colour the interface ever produces.

    
You dialled this to the Klein end yourself, so that is where it now sits: true International Klein Blue rather than the greened version. The slider stays, because a case study may want to shift a few degrees, but the house colour is the real thing.

      03
      
Texture

      Grain, dots, ghost
    

    
Four layers, all live on this page right now. First, film grain. Generated per pixel on a canvas, monochromatic, the same operation as Add Noise in Photoshop, with the tile regenerated several times a second so it scintillates instead of sitting still. The earlier version used an SVG turbulence filter, which makes soft clouds rather than grain, and you were right that it read wrong.

    
Second, a dot screen. You asked for the black to carry little dots the way your Betteride prints do, so there is a fine halftone laid over everything at low strength. It sits under the grain, so the two read as one surface rather than two effects.

    
Third, the tree. Your drawing is redrawn into the background out of actual characters, so it reads as real ASCII rather than a dot screen. Denser ink takes a heavier glyph, the same ramp the opening uses. Your Nothing page has a dot grid, and a dot grid is wallpaper: this is a picture that happens to be made of type, which is the whole argument again at another resolution. Dots remain as an option if you want the quieter version.

    
Fourth, the ghost field. Your Nothing page uses a dot grid and you wanted to go further than dots, so the background is not a pattern at all: it is one of your own images rendered as characters and held at a few per cent. It reads as texture until you look straight at it and realise it is a picture. Your ASCII tree is one of the sources, since you drew it, and it is the most literal version of the whole argument: a picture that is already made of characters.

    
    
This is the reference the whole sheet is working from, and you made it before any of this started. Black and white photography, a subject redrawn in characters, flat blue blocks, and two small windows of real colour left in. Every rule in Plate 12 is just that picture written down.

It means the texture belongs to this archive and could not be lifted onto anyone else's.

      05
      
Buttons

      Pick one, it applies everywhere
    

    
A rectangle with a fill is the default any framework hands you, and you were right to reject it. These seven come from objects rather than from a UI kit. Clicking one applies it to every button on this page, including the switcher itself, so you can judge it in use rather than in isolation.

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    

    

      

        
01Stamp

        
Hand set and never quite square: each one sits at a slightly different angle, and selecting it lands the ink at a different angle again. The fill carries the same grain as the page, so it breaks up the way a rubber stamp does on rough paper.

      

      

        
02Punch

        
A filing card with holes down the edge. The choice is a physical record rather than a highlight, which is what a card index does and what an archive is for.

      

      

        
03Terminal

        
Block inversion and a prompt character. Nothing fades because a character cell is either lit or it is not. The most literal reading of the ASCII thesis and the cheapest to build.

      

      

        
04Dymo

        
Embossed label tape: letters pressed up out of dark plastic, wide letterspacing, a highlight along the top edge and a shadow along the bottom. The most object-like of the seven.

      

      

        
05Cut

        
A scrap of photocopied paper taped down, crooked, with the grain multiplied over it. This is the zine one, and it is the only treatment that brings paper into an otherwise black interface.

      

      

        
06Crop and Resolve

        
The two worth keeping from the previous set. Crop is registration marks pulling outward. Resolve is the scramble you liked, still here, though it is the most modern of the seven because it depends on animation to work.

      

    

    
The thing that made the old set feel machine made was not the shapes, it was the easing. Every one of them faded and slid on a cubic bezier, which is the house style of every framework and every generated interface. None of these do. A stamp lands, a key bottoms out, a punch goes through: physical things snap, so every state change here is instant and the only movement is a hard one pixel shift.

    
Set to Stamp. It is the least like a web control of the seven, it puts the page's own grain inside the button, and the uneven angles mean no two sit the same way, which is the opposite of a generated grid. Cut is the one to try if you want it more zine and less print shop.

Google

      Its own zone, coming soon
    

    
This is the part the old site had no answer for. You are an Associate Product Marketing Manager on the Chrome browser team, currently working on Gemini in Chrome, and none of that appears anywhere. It should, and it should not be squeezed into the archive's language.

    
So Chrome gets its own zone, the same way Aube and Betteride do, and it is the furthest from the house style on purpose: white ground, everything centred, Google Sans, and a blue pill. Landing on it from a black photocopy archive should feel like stepping into a different building. That contrast is the argument that you can work in more than one register.

An archive has a length

    
The thing that separates an archive from a portfolio is that it accretes. As built, this site would be byte identical in six months, which makes it a brochure. Two changes fix that, and neither is expensive.

    
First, every frame carries two dates: when it was made, and when it was added. The contact sheet then orders by added, newest first, so the index is a record of the archive growing rather than an arbitrary grid. Within a group it falls back to when it was made, which is how a roll of film is ordered anyway.

Wiring it to Are.na

        
You already keep the channel. A build step reads it with a token, diffs it against a committed manifest, and any new block becomes a new frame with its added date set automatically. Adding to Are.na is then the only thing you ever have to do to update the site.

        
The catch is that Are.na v3 blocks anonymous reads, so it needs a token held as a build secret rather than in the page. That is the whole cost: one environment variable and a script.

      

      

        
Two to look at again

        
Frame 01 is your screenshot of Polsia and frame 37 is a generated image. Neither is your work, so both stay out of an archive that argues everything in it is yours.

        
Frame 15 is the closest and most personal thing in the export, and it is of someone else. You said publish everything and that is your call, but that one is worth an actual conversation rather than a default.

Betteride at ten frames has enough material for a real case study rather than the three flyers currently planned, and the Nothing set at six covers the whole campaign.

Photography is the group you flagged, and it is the one the old site had no room for at all. All fourteen frames from the export, at their own weight rather than as an appendix:

    

        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
    

    
Eleven of those are Lisbon, shot in one run, which is why they hold together as a set rather than a scatter of travel pictures. That makes photography the largest single group in the archive, larger than Betteride at ten. It should get its own route rather than living inside the general archive.

    
Symbiosis is out. Pangaia was the one entry that was a student brief for a brand you have no relationship with, and next to Aube, Betteride and Chrome it was the weakest claim on the page. Its four frames stay in the export but the route is gone. Say the word and it comes back in five minutes.

    
Two of the remaining zones are stricter than they look. Nothing keeps your original grey and its 32px dot grid rather than being redrawn in the archive's language, because that page already works and rebuilding it would only make it more like everything else. Betteride runs two palettes: the amber and near black of the site you built, and the violet and acid of the printed flyers. The case study should carry both rather than pick one.

    
The Aube card is rebuilt against the live site rather than its stylesheet, and I had it wrong before. Aube is dark, not light: warm near black, amber, Abacaxi at a light weight, and that scattered field of failure modes behind the headline. Tokenism, stereotyping, translation error, gesture misread. That drifting field is the page's whole idea, the product's problem space made visible before it says a word, and it is the thing worth carrying into the case study.

  


  
  

    

      12
      
Rules

      What holds it together
    

    
    
      
  • No linesNothing is separated by a rule or a border. Hierarchy comes from scale, space, and Klein blocks. If two things need dividing, they need more space.
    • 
      
  • Reduction is the house styleGrain, character field, ASCII opening. All three are the same move at different strengths.
    • 
      
  • Photographs keep their colourThe interface is monochrome so the images do the colouring. The only colour the interface adds is Klein.
    • 
      
  • Klein is a fieldNever a 2px underline or a small icon tint. Blocks, or not at all.
    • 
      
  • One motion idea, applied everywhereThings resolve from characters into themselves. The site opens that way and every case study enters that way, so it is a grammar rather than an intro. Nothing else moves: no page transitions, no orbs, no custom cursor.
    • 
      
  • Metadata stays visibleFrame number, title, type. Never hidden behind a hover.
    • 
      
  • Nothing easesState changes are instant. Easing is what makes an interface read as generated, and every physical thing this archive refers to, a stamp, a punch, a key, arrives in one move.
    • 
      
  • Controls are objects, not rectanglesButtons are stamps, filing cards, label tape or taped paper. The smallest element on the site is built from the same world as the largest.
    • 
      
  • Zones are allowed to disagreeThe landing page is where this language goes furthest. Project pages keep their own: Chrome is light and rounded, Aube is warm black and amber, Betteride is violet and acid. The archive holds them, it does not flatten them.
